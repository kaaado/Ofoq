# Project Context & Market Opportunity — OFOQ (أفق)

## 1. Executive Summary

**OFOQ (أفق)** is a digital ecosystem designed for the Algerian market to connect children experiencing communication or speech difficulties and autistic children with their parents and qualified professionals.

By combining an intuitive, autism-friendly mobile experience for children and families with a structured web portal for professionals and administrators, OFOQ provides continuity of support between specialized therapy sessions and daily domestic routines.

OFOQ is **not** a diagnostic instrument, medical therapy system, or institutional center management software. It is a connected ecosystem focused on Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC), personalized child spaces, and coordinated multidisciplinary collaboration.

---

## 2. The Algerian Ecosystem Reality & Problem Space

In Algeria, families supporting children with autism or speech/communication difficulties navigate distinct operational and social challenges:

### 2.1 The Continuity Gap Between Therapy and Home
Specialized therapy sessions (speech therapy / *orthophonie*, psychomotor therapy, clinical psychology, specialized education) are typically limited to 1–2 hours per week. 
- **The Core Problem:** Progress made during professional sessions is frequently decoupled from daily home life. Parents often lack clear, actionable guidelines on how to reinforce specific communication targets, vocabulary, or behavioral routines at home.
- **Hypothesis to Validate:** *Structured digital coordination between therapists and parents will measurably increase home routine consistency and child engagement.*

### 2.2 Lack of Culturally and Linguistically Adapted Tools
The majority of existing AAC and communication software is imported from North American or European markets.
- **Language Mismatch:** Imported tools rarely provide high-quality Modern Standard Arabic or Algerian dialect (Derja) speech output or iconography reflecting local foods, household items, schools, and cultural routines.
- **Accessibility Barriers:** High subscription fees denominated in foreign currency (EUR/USD), lack of international credit card access in Algeria, and complex English-only configuration create substantial friction for local parents and public practitioners.
- **Hypothesis to Validate:** *An Arabic-first, culturally familiar AAC tool with localized pictograms and audio will achieve higher daily adoption than imported alternatives.*

### 2.3 Professional Practice Overload & Coordination Friction
Independent therapists and center practitioners in Algeria manage heavy caseloads with paper-based files, disconnected messaging apps (e.g. WhatsApp/Viber), and ad-hoc verbal exchanges with parents.
- **Coordination Void:** Disorganized communication channels create privacy risks, lost observation notes, and administrative fatigue for therapists.
- **Hypothesis to Validate:** *Professionals will adopt a dedicated web workspace that centralizes parent communication, activity assignment, and progress observation logging for their assigned cases.*

## 4. Target Ecosystem Stakeholders

1. **Children with Communication Difficulties:**
   - Need clear, predictable, non-overstimulating digital spaces (**Child Worlds**).
   - Require accessible, customizable AAC communication boards and motivating activities.
2. **Parents & Families (B2C):**
   - Need a single family account to manage one or multiple children independently.
   - Require practical home guidance, localized learning resources (OFOQ Academy), and direct collaboration with their child's specialists.
3. **Professionals & Specialized Centers (B2B):**
   - Speech-language therapists (*orthophonistes*), psychomotor therapists, special educators, and clinical psychologists.
   - Need structured tools to assign target vocabulary, monitor home activity completion, and log formal observations within their professional scope.
4. **Platform Administrators:**
   - Ensure institutional governance, verify professional credentials, moderate community content, and uphold data privacy standards.

## 5. Unvalidated Market & Commercial Hypotheses

*The following commercial and operational assumptions are recorded for validation during Phase 01/02 and must not be treated as confirmed facts:*

1. **Willingness to Pay (B2C):** Preliminary models proposed family subscriptions at 1,500 DZD/month or 12,000 DZD/year. Empirical research is required to assess family price sensitivity across socio-economic strata.
2. **Willingness to Pay (B2B):** Preliminary models proposed professional subscriptions at 5,000 DZD/month. Therapist willingness to invest in software depends on demonstrable time savings and center adoption.
3. **Payment Infrastructure:** Viability of domestic payment integration via Edahabia (Algérie Poste), CIB interbank cards, and BaridiMob, versus cash or voucher distribution networks.
4. **Device Accessibility:** Device distribution across Algerian households (smartphone vs tablet ownership among target families).

---

## 6. Cultural, Privacy & Safeguarding Principles

- **Family Primacy & Privacy:** In Algerian society, family domestic life is a protected sanctuary (*el-horma*). Child data, developmental delays, and personal routines require the highest standard of confidentiality.
- **Algerian Personal Data Protection Law 18-07:** All personal and developmental information concerning children must be processed with explicit parental consent, strict access boundaries, and complete tenant isolation.
- **Non-Diagnostic, Non-Stigmatizing Stance:** OFOQ strictly avoids clinical labels, diagnostic scores, or medical claims. The product honors the child's pacing and celebrates individualized progress.
